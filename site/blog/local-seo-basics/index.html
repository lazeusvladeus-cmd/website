Local SEO basics: a practical checklist for small businesses

Google Business Profile, reviews, consistent details and location pages: the local SEO work that actually moves the needle.

VBy VladyslavPublished 17 September 20265 min read

If customers come to your premises, or you travel to them within a defined area, local search is probably one of your most valuable marketing channels. When someone searches “physio near me” or “hair salon in [town]”, Google shows a map with a short list of businesses before the regular results. Being in that list is worth a lot, and it doesn't cost anything per click.

Local SEO is the work of earning that visibility. It's less mysterious than it sounds. Most of it comes down to giving Google complete, accurate and consistent information, and giving customers good reasons to choose you. This checklist covers the essentials.

How local rankings work, in plain terms

Google describes three main factors for local results:

  • Relevance: how well your business matches what someone searched for.
  • Distance: how far your business is from the searcher, or from the location in their search.
  • Prominence: how well known and trusted your business is, based on signals such as reviews, links and mentions across the web.

You can't change where your customers are standing. But you can make your relevance unmistakable and steadily build prominence.

Step 1: Get your Google Business Profile right

Your Google Business Profile is the single most important local SEO asset. Claim and verify it if you haven't, then work through the details.

  • Primary category. Choose the most specific category that describes your core business (“Cosmetic dentist” rather than just “Dentist” if that's what you primarily do). This has a big influence on which searches you appear for.
  • Secondary categories. Add other relevant categories, but only ones that genuinely apply.
  • Services and products. List what you offer with short descriptions. These help Google match you to more specific searches.
  • Hours. Keep regular and holiday hours accurate. Being listed as open when you're closed costs trust.
  • Description. Explain what you do, who you serve and what makes you different, in natural language. Don't stuff it with keywords.
  • Photos. Add real photos of your premises, team, work and products, and keep adding them over time.
  • Booking and website links. Link to the most relevant page, and add a booking link if your system supports it.

If you're a service-area business that visits customers rather than receiving them, such as a plumber or mobile hairdresser, Google's guidelines say you should hide your address and set a service area instead.

Step 2: Build a steady stream of genuine reviews

Reviews influence both rankings and whether people choose you once they see you. What matters is not just how many you have, but how recent they are and how you respond.

  • Ask consistently. The best moment is right after a good experience. A short message with a direct link to your review form works better than a general “leave us a review sometime”.
  • Make it easy. Use the review link from your Business Profile, and consider a QR code at reception or on receipts.
  • Reply to every review. Thank people for positive ones. Respond calmly and helpfully to negative ones; future customers read your replies.
  • Never fake or buy reviews. Google's policies prohibit fake reviews and offering incentives in exchange for reviews. Getting caught can cost you your listing's credibility, or the reviews themselves.

Step 3: Keep your business details consistent everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number (often called NAP) should be identical across your website, Google Business Profile, social media and online directories. Inconsistent details, such as an old address on a directory or a different phone number on Facebook, create doubt about which information is correct.

  • Search for your business name and old addresses or phone numbers to find outdated listings.
  • Update or claim listings on the main directories in your country and industry.
  • Pick one format for your name and address and use it everywhere.

Directory listings (citations) won't transform rankings on their own, but inconsistent ones can hold you back.

Step 4: Make your website support local search

Your website gives Google context about what you do and where. A few improvements make a big difference:

  • One page per core service. A single “Services” page listing everything is hard to rank. A dedicated page for each main service, explaining it properly, gives Google a clear match for specific searches.
  • Location pages if you serve several areas. If you have multiple branches, or serve distinct towns, create a page for each with genuinely useful local information: directions, parking, the team there, local projects. Avoid near-identical pages with only the town name swapped. Google treats those as low value.
  • Clear contact details. Name, address and phone number on every page, usually in the footer, with a click-to-call link on mobile.
  • LocalBusiness structured data. Schema markup helps search engines understand your business details. It's a technical step, but a quick one for a developer.
  • Mobile speed. Most local searches happen on phones. A slow, awkward mobile site loses people even if you rank.

Step 5: Earn local mentions and links

Prominence grows when other local organizations reference you. Good sources include local business associations, suppliers and partners, sponsorships of local clubs or events, local news coverage, and industry bodies. These are links you earn by being part of your community, and they're far safer than anything you can buy.

Step 6: Measure what matters

  • Business Profile performance. Google shows how many people viewed your profile, requested directions, called or clicked to your website.
  • UTM-tagged website link. Add tracking parameters to the website link on your profile so visits from it show up separately in Google Analytics.
  • Search Console. See which searches your website appears for and how often people click.
  • Ask new customers. “How did you find us?” remains one of the most useful questions in marketing.

A 30-day local SEO plan

  1. Week 1: Claim and verify your Business Profile; fix categories, hours, services and description.
  2. Week 2: Upload fresh photos; set up a simple review request process and reply to existing reviews.
  3. Week 3: Audit your business details across directories and social profiles; fix inconsistencies.
  4. Week 4: Improve your top service page, add click-to-call and structured data, and add a tracking link to your profile.
