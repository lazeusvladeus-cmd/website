/ THE REAL PROBLEMS

Why marketing feels hard in this industry.

High ticket sizes and long decision times change how campaigns should be built and judged.

  1. 01

    Lots of leads, few real projects

    Cheap lead forms attract tyre-kickers, people with no budget, or projects outside your area. Qualification has to be built into the campaign, not left to the sales call.

  2. 02

    Months between click and contract

    A renovation or property purchase can take months to decide. Judging campaigns on this month's signed contracts misses most of the pipeline.

  3. 03

    Portfolio that doesn't sell

    Finished-project photos rarely show the process, the team or the problem solved. Prospects want to trust you with their money and their home.

  4. 04

    Seasonality and capacity

    Demand rises and falls with weather and the market, while your crew capacity is fixed. Marketing should speed up and slow down with your schedule.

  5. 05

    Housing ad restrictions

    Meta classifies housing ads as a special category, which limits targeting by age, gender and narrow location. Campaigns must be built for those rules from the start.

  6. 06

    Social media that goes nowhere

    Posting finished jobs occasionally doesn't build momentum. A consistent presence that shows progress and expertise does.

/ CHANNELS THAT FIT

Channels that suit high-value projects.

The mix differs between trades and contractors, developers selling units, and agents generating seller or buyer leads.

Google Search

CORE FOR TRADES

Captures active demand: “kitchen renovation [city]”, “builder near me”, “roof repair”. High intent, but needs careful negatives to avoid DIY and job seekers.

Meta Ads

CORE FOR DEVELOPERS & AGENTS

Project and property showcases, video walkthroughs and lead forms with qualifying questions. Built within Meta's housing special ad category rules where they apply.

Social media management

TRUST BUILDER

Progress updates, before-and-after, team and process content. Prospects research you for weeks; your profile should reassure them.

SEO & local SEO

LONG-TERM

Service and area pages plus a strong Google Business Profile bring steady enquiries without per-click costs.

CRM & nurture automation

ESSENTIAL

Long cycles need follow-up: email sequences, reminders for sales staff, and retargeting audiences built from your CRM.

YouTube / video retargeting

SUPPORTING

Longer walkthroughs and project stories for people who have already shown interest.

/ EXAMPLE CAMPAIGN STRUCTURES

Example setups for builders and developers.

These are illustrative frameworks showing how we'd typically organize an account, not results from a specific client. Your actual structure depends on budget, offer and market.

Renovation contractor: search + proof

Capture active demand on Google, use Meta to keep your work in front of people who are still deciding.

  • Google Search: Service campaigns
    • Kitchen / bathroom / extension / loft as separate ad groups
    • Service-area targeting matched to where you'll actually work
    • Negatives: DIY, jobs, salary, course, cheap (as relevant)
  • Meta: Project showcase (lead form)
    • Video walkthrough or before/after carousel
    • Form asks project type, rough budget band, timeline, postcode
  • Retargeting
    • Website visitors + video viewers, 60–90 days
    • Process, guarantees, reviews, FAQ content

Residential developer: launch funnel

Build a waitlist before launch, then convert it to viewings. Housing ad rules applied throughout.

  • Phase 1: Awareness (pre-launch)
    • Meta video: location, lifestyle, floor plans
    • Special ad category: housing (broad location radius, no age/gender targeting)
  • Phase 2: Register interest
    • Lead form or landing page with unit type and budget
    • Google Search on development name + area searches
  • Phase 3: Viewings
    • Retarget registrants with availability updates
    • CRM sequences to book viewings; sales team alerts
