/ BUYER BEHAVIOR

How Dutch customers decide.

Generalizations only go so far, but a few patterns consistently shape how campaigns should be written and built for Dutch audiences.

Directness beats hype

Superlatives and vague promises (“the best”, “revolutionary”) tend to fall flat. Clear statements of what you offer, what it costs and why it's worth it perform better. We write copy that sounds like a straight answer.

Reviews and trust marks matter

Shoppers check Google reviews and Dutch review platforms such as Kiyoh before buying, and webshops often display trust marks like Thuiswinkel Waarborg. Surfacing that proof in ads and on landing pages lowers hesitation.

Price transparency is expected

Hidden shipping costs or prices that change at checkout lose sales quickly. For consumer offers, show all-in prices including BTW (VAT) and be upfront about delivery and returns.

Dutch first, English for some audiences

English proficiency is high, but most consumers still respond better to Dutch-language ads and landing pages, especially for local services. English can work well when targeting the large international communities in cities like Amsterdam, The Hague, Rotterdam and Eindhoven.

/ LANGUAGE, CURRENCY & SEASONALITY

Euros, iDEAL and a calendar with its own peaks.

  • Currency: run ad accounts and landing pages in EUR. Choose the ad account currency carefully at setup, as changing it later is a hassle.
  • Payments: iDEAL is the default online payment method for many Dutch shoppers. Ecommerce stores without it at checkout lose buyers.
  • Language: for Dutch-language campaigns we work from your approved Dutch copy or arrange native-speaker translation. Nothing goes live without your review.
  • School holidays: the Netherlands staggers summer and spring holidays across three regions (north, middle, south), so demand for family-related offers shifts by region and week.

/ PLATFORMS & COMPLIANCE

GDPR (AVG), cookies and Dutch ad rules.

The Netherlands has an active data protection regulator and consumers who notice when consent is handled badly. We build campaigns on solid compliance foundations.

AVG / GDPR

In Dutch, the GDPR is the AVG. The regulator is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens. Lead forms need a clear purpose, a link to your privacy policy, and data handled only as described.

Cookie consent

Under the Dutch Telecommunications Act, tracking and marketing cookies need prior consent, and the Dutch regulator has taken the view that cookie walls blocking access unless visitors accept don't meet the rules. We set up consent banners with an honest “reject” option.

Google Consent Mode v2

Required for using Google's ad personalization and measurement features with EEA users. We configure it so conversion modelling can still work when users decline.

Meta Conversions API

Server-side tracking makes measurement more resilient to browser restrictions, but it must respect the same consent choices as the browser pixel.

Nederlandse Reclame Code

The Dutch advertising code sets standards for truthful, non-misleading ads, with specific rules for areas like alcohol, health claims and advertising to children.

Platform mix

Google dominates search. Facebook skews older and Instagram younger; LinkedIn is strong for B2B; Marktplaats and bol are important for some local and retail categories.

This is practical marketing guidance, not legal advice. For questions about your specific obligations, speak to a qualified adviser in the Netherlands.
