/ BUYER BEHAVIOR

How UK buyers behave.

A few traits of the UK market that change how we write ads and build funnels.

Reviews are part of the decision

UK consumers lean heavily on Google reviews and Trustpilot, and tradespeople are often found through platforms such as Checkatrade, MyBuilder or Rated People. Ads that point to genuine review profiles convert better than ones that just make claims.

“Near me” and postcode-level intent

Local searches are highly specific, often down to the town or postcode area. Service areas in Google Ads and landing pages should match where you actually work, not a vague region.

Scepticism of the hard sell

Overclaiming and pushy urgency tend to backfire. A clear offer, a bit of personality, and honest pricing guidance usually beat “LIMITED TIME ONLY” banners.

London is its own market

Click costs, competition and buyer expectations in London differ from the rest of the country. We often split London from other regions so one doesn't distort the other's results.

/ LANGUAGE, CURRENCY & SEASONALITY

Pounds, UK English and a distinct calendar.

  • Currency & VAT: ad accounts and pricing in GBP. Consumer prices should include VAT (20% standard rate) so there are no surprises at checkout.
  • Language: UK spelling and phrasing in ads and landing pages (colour, enquiry, postcode, mobile). Small details signal that you're a credible local option.
  • Dates differ from the US: Mother's Day in the UK (Mothering Sunday) falls in March, not May. US-centric marketing calendars get this wrong every year.
  • Bank holidays: long weekends shift search patterns for home improvement, travel and hospitality. We plan budgets around them.

/ PLATFORMS & COMPLIANCE

UK GDPR, PECR and the CAP Code.

Since Brexit, the UK runs its own data protection regime that closely mirrors the EU's, plus specific rules for cookies and electronic marketing.

UK GDPR & Data Protection Act 2018

The UK's version of GDPR governs how you collect and use personal data from leads and customers. The regulator is the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).

PECR: cookies

The Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations require consent for non-essential cookies such as advertising pixels. The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 is relaxing some rules for low-risk analytics in stages, so we check current ICO guidance when setting up tracking.

PECR: email & SMS marketing

Marketing to individuals generally needs consent, with a “soft opt-in” for existing customers who bought similar products and were given a chance to opt out. We build lead forms and automations around this.

ASA & CAP Code

The Advertising Standards Authority enforces the CAP Code: claims must be substantiated, prices clear, and special rules apply to health, finance and ads seen by children.

Google Local Services Ads

Available for eligible service categories in the UK, with Google screening. Worth testing for some trades alongside standard Search campaigns.

Platform mix

Google dominates search; Facebook and Instagram reach broad demographics; TikTok is strong with under-35s; LinkedIn for B2B.

This is practical marketing guidance, not legal advice. For questions about your specific obligations, speak to a qualified adviser in the UK.
